What changes when AES moves off the processor

The same 128-bit block, encrypted two ways: as 2,702 operations on a general-purpose core, and as 10 cycles in a dedicated datapath. Accelerator cycle counts are measured in RTL simulation; software cost is counted from an instrumented implementation, not estimated.

What is measured, what is not

Provenance matters more than the headline ratio. Nothing below is an unsourced estimate, and the quantities that could not be measured on this machine are named rather than guessed.

Measured — accelerator cycles

11, 10 and 1 cycles per block come from Icarus simulations of the RTL, asserted on every one of 1,013+ blocks per core, not sampled. The testbenches also verify 1,013 NIST-derived ciphertexts per run.

Measured — software cost

2,702 and 1,004 operations per block are counted from an instrumented AES that is asserted against the FIPS-197 vector, so a miscounted implementation is also a wrong one.

Derived — throughput

Bits ÷ cycles × frequency. Exact arithmetic on the measured cycle counts; no judgement involved beyond the frequency each side is clocked at.

Not verified — 384.6 MHz

Vivado is not installed on the machine these numbers were produced on, so the accelerator clock is the project's target, not a synthesis result. A full AES round between registers on a −1 Artix-7 will plausibly close nearer 150–250 MHz; scale the Gbps figures linearly if it does.

Not measured — area and power

LUT, flip-flop and power figures are absent rather than estimated. The pipelined core instantiates 200 S-boxes and will not fit the project's <3% LUT budget; the iterative cores plausibly will.

The interface becomes the bottleneck

At 1 Gbps a memory-mapped handshake costs more processor time than the core has. Past roughly 100 Mbps the accelerator needs a DMA or stream interface, or the offload gains are spent moving data.

Software baseline: MicroBlaze-class 32-bit soft core, 100 MHz, IPC 1.0. Context point — the same OpenSSL AES-128-ECB with AES-NI on the host (AMD Ryzen 5 5600H) measured 14.97 Gbps, between the overlapped and pipelined cores.
